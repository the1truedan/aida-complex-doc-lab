Same-day care packets are too dense to re-read under stress. This lab structures them into reviewable markdown — and never puts the chart in git.

why this exists

Nine notes. Fifteen months. Meeting in the morning.

Family caregivers get multi-document packets hours before a care-system meeting. Re-reading everything is the failure mode. Structure the pile. Leave the bodies out of the public tree.

harness

Synthetic triage

A runnable ADA-ish scorer on invented visit notes. Stdlib. No network. Fixtures are fake people on purpose.

proof

Scrubbed metrics

One live packet, file-timestamp proof only: counts, sizes, scores, wall clock. No names. No snippets. No hashes of the real PDFs.

boundary

PHI stays out

Legal names, MRNs, visit text, synthesis bodies, screenshots — never commit. Agents do not walk medical storage “for context.”

honest limit

Prepare-only

Not a PTSD product. Not a clinical decision system. Not a certified Section 508 auditor. A human still signs off.

A.I.D.A. is the middle of a longer document chain.

This lab exercises first-pass structure and ADA-ish triage. The rest of the mesh stays in the private control repo.

how it came to be

The packet arrived. The meeting was in the morning.

Home-lab caregiving work kept hitting the same wall: a stack of multi-page notes, not enough hours, and agents that would cheerfully commit the chart if you let them.

A.I.D.A. grew as prepare-only structure between raw intake and audit. This public lab freezes a scrubbed production story so the next session does not rediscover the problem by walking medical storage.

4–5 August 2026 — live nine-PDF first pass, local-only, ~19 minutes extract to brief. Early August 2026 — public lab with synthetic fixtures. 12 August 2026 — v0.1.0. 16 August 2026 — this page.

Future public extracts (ada-doc-check, plain-language-relay) can ship without clinical bodies. This repo feeds them honesty, not a substitute product.
